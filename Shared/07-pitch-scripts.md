# Shared - Pitch Scripts (Human-Voice Swipe File)
**Use this file when:** a human-voice starting point is needed for a cold message, to adapt with real specifics.
Governed by Master Control Rules. No em dash in any output.
Every example below sells the solution, never the person: name a real problem, give a real insight away, end on a low-pressure question, written the way an actual person talks, not marketing copy.

**Hard rule**: these are voice references, not templates. Copy the rhythm and tone, never the exact wording twice. Every bracketed detail must be swapped for something real and specific to that exact person or situation. Sending the same phrasing twice is a template again, and it reads as one.

---

### Cold Email Opener
Example 1 (B2B founder, specific bug):
> Subject: the checkout drop-off on [Product]
>
> Hey [Name], was poking around [Product] earlier and noticed checkout stalls for a second right after adding a shipping address, looks like it's re-validating the whole form instead of just the one field. Small thing, but on volume that's probably costing a chunk of completed orders every week.
>
> Happens a lot with apps that grew fast without a pass for this exact kind of state bug. Usually a day or two to actually trace and fix properly, not just patch around it.
>
> Not trying to sell you anything, just noticed it and figured you'd want to know. Happy to point you to exactly where it's happening if useful.

Example 2 (B2C solo builder, vibecoded risk):
> Subject: quick one on [App]
>
> Hey [Name], saw your post about [App] hitting [X] users, that's awesome. Took a quick look and noticed a few spots that look like they were built without a security pass, specifically around how auth tokens get handled. Not urgent-urgent, but exactly the kind of thing that becomes a real problem once you're at scale.
>
> If you ever want a second pair of eyes on it before it becomes an issue, happy to walk you through what I saw, no charge for the conversation.

### Cold DM Opener (Instagram / Facebook)
Example 1 (after engaging with their content first): "Hey! Been seeing your Reels about building [Product], really like how transparent you are about the process. Noticed a small thing in the [feature] flow when I tried it, mind if I send a quick screen recording of what I saw?"

Example 2: "Hey, loved the post about hitting [milestone]. Curious, who's handling the backend security side as you scale? Ran into a similar pattern on a few other apps around that stage and might be worth comparing notes."

### Fiverr Buyer Request Reply
> Read through your request, sounds like the module you're describing breaks specifically when [X] happens, probably a state management issue rather than the UI itself. Fixed a handful of similar cases on [platform type] apps.
>
> Happy to hop on a quick call first to confirm exactly what's going on before quoting anything, want to make sure the fix actually holds instead of just patching the symptom.

### Discovery / First Reply Opening Line
> Thanks for reaching out. Before anything else, when you say it's "slow," is that on load, on a specific action, or both? Trying to figure out if this is a database issue or a frontend one before we go further.

---

### Follow-Up (non-pushy)
> No worries if the timing's off. One more thing I noticed while looking at [X] again: [new specific insight]. Figured it might be useful either way.

---

### Comment on Someone Else's Post
> This is such a common one. Usually when I see "[symptom]" the actual root cause is [specific pattern], not [the thing everyone assumes]. Worth checking [specific thing] before doing a full rebuild.

---

### Why These Work
Every example names a specific, real-sounding detail before saying anything else, gives away the actual insight instead of teasing it, and ends on an easy, low-pressure question or offer, never a pitch. Before sending any adapted version, read it out loud, if it sounds like marketing copy or repeats a phrase from these examples, rewrite it in your own words.
