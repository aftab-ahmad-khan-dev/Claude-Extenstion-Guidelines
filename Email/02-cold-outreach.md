# Email - Cold Outreach
**Use this file when:** writing a cold email, connection note, or cold DM to a new lead.
Governed by Master Control Rules (see 01-overview.md). No em dash in any output.
Apply Shared/01-indirect-selling-framework.md to every message below. Covers the three channels used to open a conversation with a stranger: cold email, connection notes, and cold DMs.

---

### Core Approach
Every cold message, whatever the channel, is written fresh based on real, specific evidence of the prospect's problem (their site, app, job post, public complaint, or recent activity).
No mass templates. No generic "hope this finds you well" openers.
Lead with the problem, not the pitch.

---

### Choosing the Channel
1. Email - best when you have a real address and the prospect is unlikely to check DMs, or the message needs more room to make the case
2. Connection note - best when the goal is to open a relationship first, not to pitch anything yet
3. Cold DM - best when the prospect is active and reachable on a platform, and a short, casual message fits better than a formal email

Never run the same prospect through more than one cold channel at once. Pick one, and only try a second channel after a reasonable silence (see Follow-Up Sequence).

---

### Subject Lines (Email)
Short, specific, and tied to the prospect's actual situation.
No clickbait, no all caps, no spam trigger words.

---

### Connection Notes
1. Reference something specific and real from their profile, recent post, or company, never a generic compliment
2. No pitch, no mention of services, no link
3. Keep it short enough to read in one glance, connection notes are character-limited on most platforms
4. Write a completely fresh note every time, never reuse the same note twice

Daily volume and platform-specific limits live in LinkedIn/04-outreach-and-engagement.md; this section governs what the note actually says.

---

### Cold DM Opening
1. Open with the same problem-first line used in email, compressed to one or two sentences
2. Skip the formal greeting, a DM reads as a real message, not a letter
3. End with a single easy question, never a pitch and never a link on the first message
4. If they already follow you or have interacted with your content, reference that specific interaction instead of opening cold

---

### Email Structure
1. One line that proves you looked at their specific situation
2. The problem stated back to them clearly and calmly
3. A precise solution insight, not a full pitch
4. One easy next step (reply, quick call, or short question)

Keep it short. Never attach files without prior user approval.

---

### Responding When They Reply (DM or Email)
1. Reply fast, cold-outreach replies lose momentum quickly if left overnight
2. Drop the outreach framing immediately, the conversation is now a real one, not a pitch
3. Ask one discovery question before proposing anything (see 03-inbound-replies.md for the full discovery-to-proposal flow)
4. If they reply but aren't ready or aren't a fit, thank them and close the loop, never keep pushing
5. Never send a generic "thanks for connecting" message with nothing real in it, every first reply must add value or ask something specific

---

### Follow-Up Sequence
Maximum 3 follow-ups per lead, spaced several days apart, on whichever single channel was used first.
Each follow-up adds new value or a new angle, never just "checking in."
Stop the sequence immediately on any reply or clear disinterest.
Only try a second channel after the full follow-up sequence on the first channel goes unanswered, and treat it as a fresh, standalone message, not a copy-paste.

---

### Daily Limits & Deliverability
Respect sending limits on every channel to protect domain, inbox, and account reputation (platform-specific caps in LinkedIn/04-outreach-and-engagement.md).
Personalize enough that no two messages in a sequence read the same.
Track opens, replies, and positive response rate weekly, broken out by channel.
