# Shared - Indirect Selling Framework
**Use this file when:** writing or reviewing any post, comment, DM, or email, on any platform.
Master Control Rules apply here: every attachment needs approval before use, sell the solution not yourself, no em dash in any output, aim for top 3% worldwide, score effectiveness after major updates, Canva free plan only, full Image Footprint Removal on every visual.
Referenced by every content, outreach, and engagement file in this repo (Fiverr, LinkedIn, Meta, Email). Read this once, apply it everywhere content is created.

---

### The One Rule
Never sell the person. Always sell the solution.
Never say "hire me," "I'm the best," or anything that centers you as the product.
Every post, comment, and message should make the reader conclude "this person clearly knows what they're doing" without ever being told that directly.

---

### The 4-Step Content Formula
Apply this to every post, comment, and outreach email:

1. **Problem** - Name a real, specific problem the reader already has or fears having, stated more clearly than they could state it themselves.
2. **Why It Fails** - Explain calmly why the common, cheap, or generic fix to that problem breaks down. This is where expertise shows without being claimed.
3. **The Better Way** - Give a genuine, correct insight into the right approach. Give away real value, not a teaser.
4. **Soft Opening** - End with a low-pressure invitation (a question, an offer to share more, "here's what I'd check first"). Never a pitch, never a hard CTA to book or buy.

Steps 1-3 do the selling. Step 4 only opens the door.

---

### Problem & Fear Bank
Rotate these, always evidence-based, never fabricated:
- Silent costs: bugs, slow load times, insecure code, broken flows quietly costing the reader money or trust right now
- Hidden risk: "vibecoded" or AI-generated apps that look finished but are unaudited, insecure, or unable to scale
- Wrong-hire risk: generic freelancers or agencies patching symptoms instead of fixing root causes
- Growth ceiling: platforms, stores, or apps that cannot scale further without a real audit or rebuild
- Falling behind: competitors who already fixed this exact problem and are pulling ahead

Use real, specific, current examples. If unsure of a number or stat, describe the pattern instead of citing a figure.

---

### Educating So They Listen
The goal of every post or comment is trust, not applause.
1. Teach one real, specific, correct thing every time. No filler "10 tips" without substance.
2. Show the thinking, not just the conclusion, so the reader learns the judgment behind it, not just an opinion.
3. Never gatekeep the actually useful part behind "DM me to learn more."

---

### Dual-Layer Targeting: B2B + B2C in One Piece
Write one piece of content that lands for both audiences without splitting into two posts.
- B2B layer (60%): frame the problem in terms of cost, risk, scale, team time, and business outcomes. Speaks to founders and managers who control budget.
- B2C layer (40%): frame the same problem in terms of frustration, wasted effort, and personal stakes. Speaks to individuals and solo builders.
- Pick problems that genuinely hit both groups. Example: "your app loses users at step 3 of onboarding" reads as a business risk to a founder and a personal frustration to a solo builder, in the same sentence.

---

### High-Ticket Positioning Signals
- Never say "affordable," "cheap," "quick fix," or invite race-to-the-bottom comparisons.
- Talk in outcomes and risk avoided, not hours worked or price.
- Show depth: root-cause thinking, tradeoffs considered, what you would deliberately NOT do. Depth signals premium expertise.
- Let anonymized, client-approved case-style stories imply the caliber of client you work with, without naming anyone.

---

### What "Indirect" Looks Like in Practice
Never write: "I fix bugs fast, message me for a quote."
Instead: explain the actual pattern of a real bug class, why it happens, why leaving it is expensive, and what a real fix requires. Let competence be the pitch.
