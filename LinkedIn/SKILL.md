# LinkedIn Client Acquisition Ops

**Diamond Version**

## Purpose

Run focused LinkedIn sessions for client acquisition through authority, valuable engagement, and genuine conversations.

Use this workflow when the user says:

* "Check LinkedIn"
* "Run my LinkedIn session"
* "LinkedIn outreach"
* "Write today's LinkedIn post"

The goal is to attract high-quality clients through useful content, thoughtful engagement, and relevant conversations.

---

# 01. Core Rules

* Always sell the **solution**, never yourself.
* Never pitch services directly in posts or comments.
* Always present one clean batch at the end of the session for human approval.
* Prefer quality over forced volume.
* Stay specific, natural, and human.
* Hit the session targets below, but never lower the quality bar just to hit a number.
* Never use em dashes or any other emphasis dashes in drafted content.
* Write naturally using commas, periods, or separate sentences instead.
* Never write anything that feels stiff, robotic, overly polished, or AI-generated.
* **Never comment on or engage with Khubaib Rasheed or Next Level Software. This is permanent and applies to every session.**

---

# 02. Session Targets

## Connection Requests

**Maximum:** 25 personalized connection requests per session.

Only candidates scoring **7 or higher** count toward the target.

## High-Value Comments

**Target:** 15 to 20 high-value comments per session.

Only posts or engagement opportunities scoring **7 or higher** count toward the target.

## Quality Rule

These are targets and ceilings, not quotas to force.

If a full search of the feed and targeted searches produces fewer than:

* 25 qualifying connection candidates
* 15 qualifying comment opportunities

Continue searching more broadly before concluding there is not enough qualified volume.

Search across:

* More keyword combinations
* More feed content
* Notifications
* Post engagers
* Relevant founders
* Relevant decision-makers
* Relevant product discussions
* Relevant niche communities

Never pad the numbers with low-fit people or posts.

Report honestly at the end:

* How many qualified candidates were found
* How many were drafted
* How many were targeted
* Why any shortfall occurred

# 07. Content Formula

Every LinkedIn post should follow this structure:

## 1. Hook

Capture attention quickly.

The hook should create curiosity or identify a recognizable problem.

## 2. Problem or Insight

Explain:

* What is happening
* Why it matters
* What most people get wrong
* What the hidden problem is

## 3. Solution or Story

Provide:

* A practical solution
* A framework
* A lesson
* A real-world observation
* A short story
* A useful implementation perspective

The content should teach rather than sell.

## 4. CTA or Question

End with a natural conversation starter.

Examples:

* Ask for the reader's experience
* Ask how they currently solve the problem
* Invite discussion
* Ask whether others have experienced the same issue

Do not turn the CTA into a service pitch.

# 08. Visual Content

## Default Format: Single-Image Poster

Create a high-polish:

**1080 × 1350 px**

poster.

### Diamond Style Structure

The poster should contain:

1. A short two-line hook
2. The second line should create a curiosity gap
3. One dominant bold headline
4. A color accent on the key phrase
5. A short subtitle
6. A row of 3 to 5 uniform icon cards
7. Each card should contain:

   * A short label
   * One line of supporting detail
8. A bottom callout bar
9. One punchy takeaway

### Visual Style

Use:

* Tight visual hierarchy
* High contrast
* Clean spacing
* Professional typography
* Strong readability
* Consistent visual language

The visual should feel premium without becoming overly decorative.
